**First, what's genuinely strong in the design:**

- The grade system (D→C→B→A) as both a rent multiplier *and* something you can lose to theft is a smart double-use of one stat — it's doing more work than Monopoly's simple house-count, without adding a new resource type. That's efficient design.
- Exam Season (Tempo 13-16 bonus payouts) is a good pacing device — it gives the game rhythm (build up → cash in) instead of Monopoly's flat, undifferentiated grind toward bankruptcy. This alone could make games feel less draggy than original Monopoly, which is a real, known weakness of the source material.
- Tying win condition to Net Worth at a fixed turn count (Tempo 64) instead of "last player standing" is a meaningful improvement — original Monopoly can run forever with two stubborn players; yours can't. That's not a small thing, that's fixing a genuine flaw in the source game.

**Now the core concern — mapping the two old mechanics:**

You're right to feel uneasy, and here's precisely why: **"pay rent" and "buy from the owner at a higher price" are two structurally different mechanics in real Monopoly, but your Proxy Test-Taker fuses them into one gambled outcome.** That fusion isn't wrong, but it's worth being deliberate about, because right now it's doing double duty:

- Original Monopoly: rent is *guaranteed and deterministic*. Buying someone else's property isn't even a core rule — it only happens through voluntary trading (both players must agree, negotiated freely).
- Your game: landing on an owned tile gives you a *choice* — pay guaranteed rent, or gamble to steal. That's actually more interesting than Monopoly's version, because Monopoly's trading is optional and rarely used by casual players. You've made "trying to take someone's property" a core, repeatable action instead of a rare negotiated event. That's a genuine design upgrade, not just a reskin.

The unease you're feeling is probably about **variance, not concept.** A gambled steal-or-pay-double-penalty mechanic swings games much harder than flat rent. Here are three ways to tune that, pick based on what kind of game you want:

**Option A — Keep it as a single risk mechanic (current design), but cap the downside.**
Keep Proxy Test-Taker as is, but change the failure penalty so it's *either* lose the bribe *or* pay rent — not both. Stacking both costs on failure is what makes it swingy enough to bankrupt someone in one bad roll. This is the smallest change and preserves your existing math.

**Option B — Split it into two separate, clearer actions.**
- *Study Material Fee (Rent)* stays exactly as you have it — deterministic, clean, always happens if you don't attempt a steal.
- *Proxy Test-Taker* becomes a genuinely separate, deliberate action a player can choose to attempt only when they have enough spare cash to survive failure — maybe restricted to once per Tempo, or with a cooldown, so it's a strategic tool rather than something rolled into every single landing. This keeps rent as your safe, predictable baseline (so the economy doesn't spiral from variance) while the steal mechanic becomes a spikier, opt-in power play — closer to how trading works in real Monopoly (rare, deliberate, high-stakes) rather than a coin-flip you face constantly.

**Option C — Make success scale continuously instead of pass/fail.**
Instead of a binary dice check, let the bribe amount buy a *partial* stake — e.g., paying 50% of tuition as bribe gives you 50% ownership of future rent from that tile (shared income) rather than all-or-nothing theft. This removes gambling swinginess entirely and turns it into a pure economic decision, at the cost of losing the fun "heist" feeling successful steals currently have.

My honest read: **Option B is probably your best fit** — it keeps the fun gambling flavor for players who want to be aggressive, but stops rent (your economy's backbone) from being entangled with a coin flip, which is likely the actual source of your unease.
